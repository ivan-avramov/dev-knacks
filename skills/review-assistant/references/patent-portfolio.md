# Someone else's patents, as part of evaluating them

Usually part of a product or vendor review: does their IP back what they sell? Google Patents' legal status is algorithmic, not a legal conclusion.

## Pass 0: problem map
What does the vendor claim the IP protects, in their words, and how central is that to the pitch ("patented technology", "proprietary")?

## Per patent
- Bibliographic data and status; separate issued from pending; treat marketing "patented" as possibly pending.
- Claim 1 in plain language.
- What it actually covers, and how that relates to what is sold.
- Examiner-cited prior art, chain of title, inventor concentration.
- Family: continuations, foreign filings.

## Across the portfolio
- Compare the vendor's patent marking and notices with what the patents claim; a product marked patented with no covering patent is a finding.
- Does it cover the differentiating mechanism, or only peripheral parts?
- Note expiry horizons relative to product life.
- Seek independent descriptions of the patented mechanism; trace each to its origin, including the vendor's own pages and deleted ones.

Grade like any evidence (see `product-vendor.md`, Grading). Claim text is primary; the vendor's description of its patents is a vendor claim.
